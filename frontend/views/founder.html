The Journey

From Operations to Engineering

My path to software engineering wasn't traditional, and that's exactly what makes it valuable.

Before writing my first line of code, I spent years leading operations at JTR Holidays, managing international ticketing workflows, coordinating cross-functional teams, and solving high-stakes operational challenges. I learned how businesses actually work—not from textbooks, but from the ground up.

That experience taught me something critical: technology should serve business goals, not the other way around.

The Transition

In 2023, I made the decision to transition into software engineering. Not because I was bored with operations, but because I saw the potential to create solutions that could transform entire workflows.

I immersed myself in learning—Python, Flutter, backend architecture, database design. But more importantly, I approached every project with the same operational mindset I'd developed in my previous career: What problem are we actually solving? What's the business impact?

The Philosophy

At DeveloperStudio, we don't just build features. We build systems that:

  • Solve real business problems, not theoretical ones
  • Scale with your growth, not hold you back
  • Communicate clearly, without technical jargon
  • Deliver on time, because your business depends on it

This isn't just about writing clean code (though we do that). It's about understanding that every line of code we write impacts someone's business, someone's customers, someone's livelihood.

Today

As the founder of DeveloperStudio, I bring together my operational experience and technical expertise to lead a team that builds software the right way—with business impact at the forefront.

We've delivered 50+ projects, from mobile apps that serve thousands of users to backend systems that process critical business operations. Every project is approached with the same question: How does this move the business forward?
